# Day 2 — First Session: Role of FIEO in Export Promotion and Government Schemes

## Overview
This session introduces **FIEO (Federation of Indian Export Organisations)** — its role as the apex body representing Indian exporters — and covers how the international **HS Code (Harmonised System)** classification works globally, along with key government export-incentive schemes. It closes with a live Q&A covering turmeric, steel handicrafts, Ayurvedic/Unani medicine, chemical/pharma products, and industrial belt exports.

## FIEO — Role and Structure
- FIEO is the **national umbrella trade body** for Indian exporters, working alongside sector-specific Export Promotion Councils.
- Functions include: promoting international trade, liaising with the government on trade policy, sharing updates on **trade barriers, exchange rate changes, and international events**, and acting as a bridge between Indian exporters and **foreign chambers of commerce / trade counterparts** abroad.
- Provides **alerts from Indian embassies and consulates** and information from foreign High Commissions (useful for identifying overseas business leads/buyers).
- Has a **membership registration** process (like RCMC) that must be **renewed annually**.
- Operates with **regional/state offices** across India — participants were given examples of state-level contacts (e.g., a contact in Chatra district, Jharkhand, near Ranchi) for local assistance.
- Maintains the **Indian Trade Portal**, a government website providing country-wise trade agreement details, duty information, and HS chapter lookups.

## HS Code — International Classification System
- The HS Code system is maintained by the **World Customs Organization (WCO)** and used by around **200 countries** for classifying traded goods.
- The **first 6 digits are internationally standardized**; individual countries can add further digits (e.g., India uses an extended 8-digit ITC-HS code) for more specific product description.
- Structure: **Chapters** (broad categories) → sub-headings → increasingly specific codes; knowing your product's correct HS chapter/code is essential for checking **import duty rates** in the destination country and for accessing trade agreement benefits.
- The Indian Trade Portal allows searching by HS chapter/code to see applicable duties and trade agreement terms for a given destination country.

## Free Trade Agreements (FTAs)
- India has signed various types of trade agreements with partner countries, including:
  - **Free Trade Agreements (FTA)**
  - **Economic Partnership Agreements (EPA)**
  - **Economic Cooperation Agreements (ECA)**
- These agreements can **reduce or eliminate import duty** for Indian goods in the partner country — checking whether an FTA exists with your target market can make your product more price-competitive.

## Government Export Incentive Schemes Mentioned
- **Duty Drawback** — refund of duties paid on inputs used in manufacturing exported goods.
- **Interest Subvention** — a subsidy on interest rates for export finance/loans, making working capital cheaper for exporters.
- Note: benefits/incentives are generally tied to correct **HS code classification** and being properly registered (RCMC) with the relevant Export Promotion Council.

## Registration & Eligibility Reminders
- **RCMC/Council registration** must match your specific product category and needs **annual renewal**.
- Basic eligibility for holding an export-import license/account typically requires a **bank account and financial records (e.g., balance sheet)**.
- Multi-product exporters may need to register with **more than one council** (e.g., a spice exporter registers with the Spices Board; a different product may fall under a different board).

## Q&A Highlights
- **Turmeric exporters**: repeatedly directed to register with the **Spices Board**, since turmeric and turmeric-based products fall under its jurisdiction; participants asked about turmeric-specific documentation and quality processes.
- **Steel handicraft items (e.g., decorative door handles)**: identified as having **strong demand in the UK**; such items fall under the **handicrafts export category**, so registration should be with the Export Promotion Council for Handicrafts.
- **Ayurvedic and Unani medicine exports**: flagged as a **distinct category** from general product exports — participants were advised these need a separate registration/regulatory process specific to Ayurveda/Unani products rather than the standard goods pathway.
- **Chemical/pharmaceutical product exporter**: asked about confirming correct registration for a chemical export business; advised to confirm the right council based on the specific chemical/product line.
- **Industrial belts to the Middle East**: a participant planning to export industrial belts to Middle Eastern countries was advised on using trade/export data resources to validate the plan.
- **General registration renewal** was reiterated multiple times as a **yearly requirement**, not a one-time step.

## Key Takeaways
- **FIEO** functions as the exporter's primary link to government policy updates, trade barrier alerts, and international trade contacts — a resource distinct from (but complementary to) product-specific Export Promotion Councils.
- Understanding **HS codes** is the gateway to checking import duties, FTA benefits, and government incentive eligibility for any product.
- **Duty drawback and interest subvention** are two concrete government schemes that reduce cost/improve margins for registered exporters.
- All export registrations (RCMC, FIEO membership) require **annual renewal** — lapses can affect scheme eligibility.
- Product category determines which specific board/council to register with — always confirm the right one before assuming a single registration covers all products.
