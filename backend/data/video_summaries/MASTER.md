# MASTER SUMMARY — EXIM Business Course (India)

## Course Overview
This course teaches the complete, practical process of starting and running an export-import (EXIM) business from India — from legally forming a company through to getting paid for an international shipment. Across 10 sessions (Days 1–6), it covers: company formation and mandatory registrations (IEC, RCMC), market research using government trade-data tools, the role of government bodies (FIEO, Export Promotion Councils) and export incentive schemes, sourcing buyers through embassies/chambers/B2B platforms, selling globally via eCommerce, digital lead-generation tools, Incoterms and shipping responsibility, international payment methods and banking, the role of Customs House Agents, and a closing session helping participants turn course knowledge into an action plan. The course draws on real participant Q&A throughout, covering products ranging from spices and turmeric to handicrafts, Ayurvedic medicine, jewelry, and industrial goods.

## The 15–20 Most Important Takeaways (Grouped by Topic)

### Company Formation
1. **Choose the right business structure** for your stage: Sole Proprietorship (fastest, needs only PAN) → Partnership (needs a Partnership Deed) → LLP/OPC/Private Limited (more credibility, more formality via MCA registration).
2. **Company naming matters**: keep it short, relevant to your product/activity, check domain-name availability, and consider trademark registration for long-term brand protection.
3. A proprietorship name can repeat across cities, but a **Private Limited company name must be nationally unique**.
4. Open a **business bank account** (PAN, Aadhaar, address proof) — choice of public vs. private bank is a service/convenience trade-off.

### Documentation & Registration
5. **IEC (Import Export Code)** is mandatory for any commercial export/import — apply online at **dgft.gov.in**, verified via Aadhaar OTP; it now requires **annual updation** to stay active.
6. **RCMC (Registration-Cum-Membership Certificate)** from the relevant sector's **Export Promotion Council** (or Commodity Board / APEDA) is required to access government benefits — match your registration to your product's actual industry, and register with multiple councils if you deal in multiple product categories.
7. **GST registration** is required for commercial billing (not for merely developing the business).
8. Know your product's correct **HS Code (Harmonised System)** — a 6-digit international standard (maintained by the World Customs Organization, used by ~200 countries) extended to 8 digits in India (ITC-HS) — this unlocks import-duty lookups, FTA benefits, and scheme eligibility.

### Government Schemes & Bodies
9. **FIEO (Federation of Indian Export Organisations)** is the apex exporter body — provides trade policy updates, trade-barrier alerts, embassy/consulate contacts, and requires annual membership renewal.
10. Government incentive schemes include **Duty Drawback** (refund of input duties) and **Interest Subvention** (subsidized export finance interest rates).
11. **Free Trade Agreements (FTA), Economic Partnership Agreements, and Economic Cooperation Agreements** can reduce/eliminate import duty in partner countries — always check if one exists with your target market.
12. Use official data tools — **commerce.gov.in, the Export-Import Data Bank, and the Niryat Mitra app** — to validate product demand, competition, and trend direction with real data, not guesswork.

### Buyer Sourcing & Marketing
13. Find buyers through multiple channels simultaneously: **Indian embassy/consulate trade promotion officers, chambers of commerce (Indian and foreign, e.g. Indo-American Chamber), B2B marketplaces (Alibaba), EPC member directories, LinkedIn, and industry-specific company databases.**
14. A credible, complete **company profile and professional website** (proper legal structure, trademark, verified contact info, SEO) is a prerequisite for buyers to trust and respond to outreach.
15. **eCommerce (e.g., Amazon Global Selling)** offers a lower-barrier path for small exporters to reach international buyers without a physical overseas presence — strongest categories: jewelry, health/beauty/Ayurvedic products, handicrafts, and agri/food items.
16. Always confirm **buyer-specific and country-specific product specifications** before finalizing a deal — requirements vary by market even for the same product.

### Logistics & Incoterms
17. **Incoterms** (regulated by the ICC, Paris) define only **delivery point, cost allocation, and risk transfer** between buyer and seller — not quality, quantity, or payment terms. The four groups run from **Ex Works (minimum seller responsibility)** to **DDP (maximum seller responsibility)**, with **FOB and CIF** the most common terms in practice.
18. Always calculate the **full landed cost** (product + margin + transport + duties + clearance charges) before quoting a price, and choose the right **container type** (FCL, LCL/consolidated, reefer, open-top) based on product size/quantity/temperature needs.
19. **Customs House Agents (CHAs)** handle the specialized documentation (Shipping Bill for exports, Bill of Entry for imports) and customs interface — compare quotes from multiple CHAs and shipping lines rather than defaulting to one. **Dry ports/ICDs** make customs accessible even for landlocked exporters.

### Payments & Banking
20. International payment methods range from lowest exporter risk (**advance payment**) to higher risk (open account), with **Documents Against Payment (D/P), Documents Against Acceptance (D/A), and Letters of Credit (LC)** as secure middle-ground options — an LC is the most secure since a bank guarantees payment.
21. Always specify **payment terms, currency, and who bears bank/cancellation charges** clearly in the contract, and ensure all trade documents (commercial invoice, packing list) are free of discrepancies, since banks and insurers check these before releasing payment. Foreign exchange transactions must comply with **FEMA**.

## Final Action Checklist — Starting an Export Business in India

1. **Choose your product and validate demand** using HS code lookups and official export-import data tools (commerce.gov.in, Export-Import Data Bank) — check which countries import it, at what volume, and the trend direction.
2. **Register your business entity** — start with a sole proprietorship if new, upgrading to LLP/Pvt Ltd as you scale; secure a matching domain name and consider a trademark.
3. **Open a business bank account** with a bank experienced in international trade transactions.
4. **Obtain your IEC** via dgft.gov.in and set a reminder to update it annually.
5. **Register for RCMC** with the Export Promotion Council/Commodity Board matching your product's actual industry use.
6. **Get GST registration** for commercial billing.
7. **Identify your product's HS code** and check destination-country import duty, labeling rules, and any applicable FTA benefits.
8. **Arrange quality/organic certification** if relevant to your product category, before approaching international buyers.
9. **Build a credible online presence**: a professional website, verified company profile, and active social media/LinkedIn presence.
10. **Identify buyers** through embassy trade officers, chambers of commerce, B2B platforms (Alibaba), EPC directories, and/or eCommerce marketplace listings (Amazon Global Selling).
11. **Understand and select the right Incoterm** (start with FOB if new; consider CIF/DDP as you gain experience) and calculate your full landed cost before quoting.
12. **Choose a reliable Customs House Agent (CHA)** and shipping line by comparing multiple quotations.
13. **Agree clear payment terms** in your contract — currency, due dates, bank charge responsibility — and prefer secured methods (advance payment, LC, D/P) especially for new buyer relationships.
14. **Prepare accurate trade documentation** for every shipment: commercial invoice, packing list, Shipping Bill/Bill of Entry, Bill of Lading.
15. **Stay connected and keep learning** — join trade networks, monitor policy updates via FIEO/government portals, and continue refining your process shipment by shipment.
