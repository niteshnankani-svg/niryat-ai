# Day 3 — First Session: Doing Small Business Globally through eCommerce (by Mr. Gaurang Vasavada, 21.07.21)

## Overview
This session, led by guest speaker Mr. Gaurang Vasavada, focuses on how small and medium exporters can reach global buyers directly through **cross-border eCommerce** (e.g., Amazon Global Selling) rather than relying only on traditional trade channels. It covers which product categories perform well online, the operational essentials (banking, payment gateways, logistics, customs documents, pricing, returns), and closes with a detailed Q&A.

## Why eCommerce for Small Exporters
- Cross-border eCommerce lets even small businesses reach international buyers **without needing a physical presence abroad** — no in-person visits, no local office required to start selling.
- Marketplaces (Amazon and similar platforms) already have the customer base, payment infrastructure, and in many cases warehousing — sellers plug into this existing system instead of building it from scratch.
- Highlighted as especially valuable for small and medium enterprises (SMEs) who cannot afford traditional overseas market-entry costs.

## Product Categories With Strong Export/eCommerce Potential
- **Jewelry** — gold, precious stone, and beaded jewelry; India has multiple manufacturing hubs (e.g., Jaipur, Surat) making this a strong online export category.
- **Health & beauty products** — including **Ayurvedic products** (e.g., agar/oudh-based items, oils), natural/herbal items, dental and skin-care products.
- **Handicrafts** — from centers like Jammu & Kashmir, artisans producing furniture, carpets, and decorative items.
- **Agriculture and food products** — natural/organic food items are noted as having rising global demand.
- **Home décor, textiles, and fashion items** — general lifestyle and apparel categories also cited as consistently in demand.
- Products from **multiple parts of India** (not just one hub) collectively feed these export categories, showing the geographic spread of India's manufacturing base.

## Setting Up to Sell Online Internationally
- **Website**: having your own branded, **mobile-friendly website** builds trust and is useful alongside marketplace listings — clean design and easy navigation increase buyer confidence and reduce hesitation/questions.
- **Marketplace accounts**: register as a seller on relevant platforms (Amazon is the primary example discussed, including **Amazon.in**, and Amazon's international/US marketplace) — separate seller accounts and requirements apply for domestic vs. international marketplaces.
- **Local requirements per market**: each country/marketplace may have different documentation, product category rules, or listing requirements — don't assume one setup works everywhere.

## Banking & Payments
- You will typically need **two types of bank accounts**: a regular domestic account and a **separate account/facility suited for international trade transactions**.
- A **strong banking partner** is important — banks differ in how well they support foreign currency transactions, so choose one experienced with export/import trade.
- **Payment gateways** are essential for receiving online payments in foreign currency; the exporter should confirm which gateways are supported and how foreign currency is credited/converted.
- Recommended to maintain proper **bank statements and foreign remittance certificates** (e.g., FIRC) for each transaction — needed for compliance, GST, and RCMC/scheme benefit claims.

## Logistics & Shipping
- Delivery/shipping options include **postal services (e.g., Speed Post, EMS/foreign post)**, **courier partners**, and **dedicated logistics/clearing-house agents**.
- **Foreign Post Offices (FPOs)** in various Indian cities (e.g., Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, and other listed cities) act as a low-cost gateway for exporting parcels/samples using postal channels — useful for small-volume or sample shipments.
- For larger or more frequent shipments, using a **courier/logistics partner** or **clearing and forwarding (C&F) agent** reduces transit time and handles customs paperwork more reliably.
- Proper **packaging and labeling** — including country-specific labeling requirements — must be handled correctly to avoid customs delays or rejected shipments.

## Customs, Duty & Documentation
- Required customs documents for shipments include a **shipping bill**, **declaration forms**, and invoice/value details.
- **Import duty** in the destination country is a key cost factor — this again ties back to knowing the correct **HS code**, since duty rates vary by product classification and by country.
- Products can face **different duty treatment** in different countries (e.g., differing rules for a product like a garment or cotton item depending on the destination), so this must be checked per market before pricing.
- Every shipment should carry accurate documentation to avoid it being held up or rejected by customs on either side.

## Pricing Strategy
- Prices should be **set separately per country/marketplace** — the same product may need a different listed price in the US vs. the UK vs. India, since costs, duties, and market expectations differ.
- Total price should factor in **product cost + shipping/logistics cost + customs duty + marketplace fees + payment gateway charges**, not just the base product cost.
- Free/discounted shipping thresholds and other marketplace-specific pricing norms should be studied for each platform.

## Returns & Customer Service
- International marketplaces often have **higher return-rate expectations** and strict customer service standards; exporters must budget for a percentage of returns and factor this into pricing/margins.
- Have a plan for handling returns (e.g., whether returned goods are sent back to India or handled locally via the marketplace's return infrastructure).

## Q&A Highlights
- **Coffee producer** asking about selling on **Amazon and Flipkart**: encouraged to explore both domestic and international marketplace listings depending on target buyers.
- **Small-town/non-metro seller (near Kolkata)**: confirmed that eCommerce export is accessible even without being based in a metro city, as long as banking and logistics partners are in place.
- Discussion on whether a **separate GST/export documentation batch** is needed for eCommerce exports vs. traditional export shipments — advised to maintain proper compliance records regardless of channel.
- Confirmed that **inventory can be stored closer to the end market** (e.g., in a foreign warehouse via the marketplace's fulfillment service) to reduce delivery time, where such fulfillment options are available.

## Key Takeaways
- **Cross-border eCommerce** is a realistic, lower-barrier path for small Indian exporters to sell internationally without needing an overseas physical presence.
- Categories with strong traction: **jewelry, health & beauty/Ayurvedic products, handicrafts, and agri/food items**.
- Success requires getting several operational pieces right together: a **professional website**, the right **marketplace accounts**, a **capable banking partner and payment gateway**, reliable **logistics/customs handling**, and **market-specific pricing**.
- Always check destination-country **import duty and labeling rules** by HS code before listing a product in a new market.
- Budget for **returns and marketplace fees** as real costs, not afterthoughts.
