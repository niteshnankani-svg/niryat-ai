# Day 2 — Second Session: Various Sources for Buyers (20.07.21)

## Overview
This session covers where and how exporters can find genuine international buyers — through official government/diplomatic channels, chambers of commerce, B2B marketplaces, and by presenting a credible company profile. It closes with a Q&A touching on pricing in foreign currency, company structure/branding, product specifications demanded by overseas buyers, and quality certification when the exporter cannot be physically present abroad.

## Government & Diplomatic Channels
- **Indian Embassies and Consulates** — each has a **Commercial Department / Trade Promotion Officer** whose role is to help Indian exporters connect with local buyers and importers in that country, share market information, and support joint ventures and technology-transfer opportunities.
- These offices can be contacted directly for country-specific buyer leads and are considered one of the most credible sources since they are backed by government relations between the two countries.

## Chambers of Commerce
- Both **Indian chambers of commerce** and their **foreign counterparts** (example cited: the **Indo-American Chamber of Commerce**) act as networking bodies connecting exporters with importers/buyers in partner countries.
- Membership in relevant chambers gives access to business directories, member lists, and networking events with potential buyers.

## B2B / B2C Marketplaces
- Global online marketplaces (**Alibaba** cited as the leading example) let exporters list products and connect directly with international buyers.
- A complete, well-maintained **company profile** — with full company details, product listings, and contact information — is essential, since buyers evaluate suppliers based on how their profile appears on these platforms.
- Tender search tools are also available on such platforms/websites, letting exporters search and respond to international **tenders** relevant to their product.

## Export Promotion Council / RCMC as a Buyer-Discovery Channel
- Being registered with your relevant Export Promotion Council (e.g., for Handicrafts) also makes you discoverable, since councils maintain **member directories** that overseas buyers/agencies may consult.
- Reinforces the importance of getting and maintaining RCMC registration — it is not just a compliance requirement but also a credibility and visibility tool.

## Company Profile & Credibility
- A strong, verifiable **company profile** (proper legal name, registered address, phone, email, website) builds buyer trust — compared to companies with vague or incomplete listings.
- Larger, well-known companies (examples cited: **Sun Pharma, Cipla**) are used to illustrate how strong branding and company credibility support winning international business, even though small exporters can compete with a clear, honest profile.
- Choice of business structure (proprietorship vs. private limited) and securing a **trademark** for your brand name were again flagged as factors that strengthen credibility with buyers over time.

## Product Specifications & Buyer Requirements
- Overseas buyers often specify precise product requirements — example discussed: a **Singapore buyer's detailed specification for red chilli** (grade, quality parameters) that the exporter must match exactly before a deal proceeds.
- Exporters should expect to adjust or confirm product specifications per buyer/per country rather than assuming one specification fits all markets.

## Pricing & Currency
- Export prices are generally quoted and settled in **foreign currency** (e.g., USD), not Indian Rupees.
- The final price should account for the **prevailing exchange rate** at the time of quotation, since currency fluctuations affect actual realized revenue.

## Certification Without Physical Presence Abroad
- A participant asked how to get **quality/organic certification or approval** for a market (e.g., in Europe) without being physically present there.
- Guidance: this is handled through **authorized local certification bodies or agents** in the destination country/region who can conduct the required inspection/certification process on the exporter's behalf, so physical presence is not mandatory.

## Warehouse Setup & Government Support
- A question on setting up a **warehouse abroad** was addressed, noting that **government grants/support schemes** exist to help Indian exporters establish overseas storage/distribution infrastructure — exporters should check with the relevant scheme/authority for eligibility.

## Import Duty as a Market Filter
- Example discussed: **mango exports**, where high **import duty** in a potential destination country can make that market unviable — reinforcing the earlier lesson (Session 2, Day 1) to check destination-country import duty via HS code before committing to a market.

## Key Takeaways
- Buyers can be found through **multiple channels**: Indian embassies/consulates (trade promotion officers), chambers of commerce, B2B platforms like Alibaba, and EPC member directories — use more than one simultaneously.
- A complete, credible **company profile** (legal structure, trademark, verified contact details) is a prerequisite for buyers to trust and engage with you.
- Always confirm **buyer-specific and country-specific product specifications** before finalizing a deal.
- Quote and settle prices in **foreign currency**, accounting for exchange rate movement.
- Certification/approval processes in a foreign market can typically be completed via **local authorized agents**, without needing to travel there.
- Government **grants/schemes** may support overseas warehouse setup — check eligibility before ruling this out as too costly.
