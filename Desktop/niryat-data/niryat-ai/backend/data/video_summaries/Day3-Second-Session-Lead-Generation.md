# Day 3 — Second Session: Lead Generation Tools (21.07.21)

## Overview
This session covers practical tools and techniques for generating international buyer leads — social media platforms, company databases, browser tools for finding contacts, and email outreach — followed by a Q&A on specific export queries (agarbatti to Dubai, cotton exports, African markets, and homeopathic medicine manufacturing).

## HSN Code & Registration Reminders (from opening Q&A)
- A participant asked about a "mouth fresheners" product with no clear HSN code — guidance was to research the closest matching HSN classification before proceeding, since correct classification is required for registration and duty purposes.
- **Tamarind kernel powder** was discussed as an example product used in **industrial applications** (not just food) — despite being sourced as an agricultural commodity, its industrial use may mean registration should be checked with **both the Spice Board and Textile Board** (or the board matching its actual end-use industry), since the appropriate EPC/RCMC depends on how the product is actually used downstream, not just its raw origin.

## Social Media as a Lead Generation Channel
- **Facebook**: business pages, groups, and paid advertisements (Facebook Ads) can be used to reach prospective buyers and build brand visibility; joining relevant industry/trade groups helps with networking and direct outreach.
- **Instagram, Twitter**: useful for visual/brand promotion and lighter-touch engagement with international audiences.
- **LinkedIn**: highlighted as a particularly active professional network for B2B lead generation — useful for identifying specific decision-makers within target companies (e.g., regional managers, marketing managers, executive directors) rather than just general company contact info.

## Company Databases & Prospecting
- Building a target list involves finding **company profiles/prospectuses** — full company details (industry, size, location, key contacts) — before reaching out, rather than cold-contacting generic addresses.
- Identifying the **right person within a company** (e.g., a regional or marketing manager rather than a generic inbox) significantly improves response rates.
- Industry-specific searches (e.g., filtering by sector — pharma, fertilizer, cosmetics, automotive) help narrow down a realistic buyer list instead of mass-contacting unrelated companies.

## Browser & Automation Tools
- **Google Chrome extensions** were mentioned as tools that can help with prospecting — e.g., finding contact/email information associated with company websites or LinkedIn profiles, saving manual search time.
- **Email marketing automation** tools were discussed for sending outreach at scale and tracking engagement, rather than manually emailing each prospect one at a time.

## Marketplaces & Q&A Platforms
- **Amazon** (seller/marketplace listings) reiterated as a channel where buyers actively search for products — maintaining a well-optimized listing supports inbound lead generation, not just direct outreach.
- **Quora** and similar Q&A/community platforms were mentioned as sources for market research and even indirect lead generation, by answering relevant questions and building credibility in a product category.

## Company Credibility for Trust-Building
- A professional company website, consistent branding, and a clear prospectus/profile were again emphasized as necessary for prospective clients to trust and respond to outreach — cold contact alone (without a credible online presence) is less effective at converting leads.

## Q&A Highlights
- **Agarbatti (incense) export to Dubai**: a buyer's specific requirement/specification for agarbatti needs to be understood and matched precisely before proceeding.
- **Cotton exports**: a brief question on cotton export was addressed, without extensive elaboration beyond general export process guidance already covered in earlier sessions.
- **African markets — reducing risk with unregulated agents**: a participant asked how to reduce risk when African-country buyers are approached through agents in markets that are "not properly regulated." Guidance: be cautious with **payment terms** — secure payment upfront or through safer payment mechanisms (e.g., LC or advance payment) before shipping, since agent-mediated deals in less-regulated markets carry higher non-payment risk.
- **Homeopathic medicine manufacturing/export**: a participant asked for help starting export of homeopathic medicines; guided toward checking specific regulatory/registration requirements applicable to medicinal products (distinct from general goods) before proceeding.
- **Gujarat pharma manufacturers (Ahmedabad)**: noted as having strong manufacturing capability, relevant to participants scouting manufacturing partners in that industry.

## Key Takeaways
- Lead generation should combine **outbound** (social media outreach, email automation, LinkedIn contact-finding) with **inbound** (marketplace listings, Quora/community engagement, SEO-friendly website) approaches.
- Always identify the **right decision-maker contact**, not a generic company address, to improve response rates.
- Match your product's registration (RCMC/EPC) to its **actual end-use industry**, not just its raw material origin.
- When dealing with buyers via agents in **less-regulated markets** (e.g., parts of Africa), prioritize secure payment terms to reduce non-payment risk.
- Medicinal products (e.g., homeopathic medicine) have **distinct regulatory requirements** separate from standard goods exports — verify these before starting.
